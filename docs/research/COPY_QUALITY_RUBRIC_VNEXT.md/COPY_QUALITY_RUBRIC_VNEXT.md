# Copy Quality Rubric vNext

Version: 3P / 2026-09-23

Copy is evaluated as customer movement, not as elegant wording in isolation.

## Axes

| Axis | Evaluation question |
|---|---|
| Customer relevance | Does the first language name a real customer situation or question? |
| Immediate comprehension | Can the reader tell what this is and why it matters without internal IA vocabulary? |
| Specificity | Is the concrete detail evidence-safe and company-specific? |
| Emotional truth | Does it describe a felt or practical tension without invented drama? |
| Psychological movement | Before → Copy Move → After is observable. |
| Natural Japanese | Word order, particles, endings, rhythm, and line breaks sound human. |
| Meaningful brevity | No sentence carries two unrelated jobs; no ornamental compression hides meaning. |
| Trust safety | Claims, boundaries, qualifications, outcomes, and unknowns are separated. |
| Visual relationship | Copy and visual divide work; neither merely repeats the other. |
| Conversion relevance | CTA matches the current decision size and states what happens next. |
| Business specificity | Generic substitution changes the meaning or weakens the proof. |

## Copy hard fails

- Internal strategy or information-architecture language appears as customer-facing copy.
- Abstract reassurance is used where a concrete boundary or next event is required.
- A customer outcome, qualification, process, price, or urgency is invented.
- Japanese line breaks isolate a particle, conjunction, single noun, or single verb.
- CTA asks for a larger commitment than the evidence and customer state justify.
- Editing one scene silently changes approved copy or meaning elsewhere.

## Minimum Copy Gate

G2 passes only when Hero, body, transition, trust/objection, and CTA each have a documented customer-state change. A page that is grammatically correct but does not change the decision state is HOLD.
